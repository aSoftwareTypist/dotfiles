## Trend Analysis

Trend is a general direction of prices on a specified time.

Demand exceeds supply, price will rise. Conversely, if supply exceeds demand, price will fall.

So these are the various peaks or tops and these are the troughs or bottoms of the trend. Trend are generally of three types. Up trend, down trend and sideways trend. 

In an uptrend peaks and troughs are higher and higher each top is higher than the previous one and each bottom is higher than the previous one.

In our downtrend there are successive lower peaks and troughs. So in a down trend we get lower tops and lower bottoms here supply is more than their demand

Besides up trend and down trend there is one more trend that is the sideways trend or we can see that market. is ranging or trend less. Here demand and supply are in balance and price movement confined between our range. These are horizontal trends and there is little movement up and down.

types of trend based on time frame

secular trend: last 5 to 20 years

major trend: >1 year, measured with week candles

Intermediate trend: 1 to 3 months, are correction in major trend, measured with day candles

minor trends: smaller trends, of days or weeks. are correction to intermediate trends. these trends can be easily seen on hourly and 15 minute time frame charts.

Trends are confined within one another long term trend consist of a series of intermediate trends and an intermediate trend consist of a series of short term trends. Trading in direction of major trend gives you more profit than trading in direction of intermediate trend and trading in the direction of intermediate trend gives you more profit than trading in short term trends.



## Gap Theory

* A **gap** is an empty area on the price chart where **no trades occurred**.
* It usually forms between one day's **close** and the next day's **open**.
* **Common cause:** news released after market close, such as earnings. Strong earnings create overnight buying demand, so the stock opens above the prior close.

### Direction

| Type         | Condition                                 |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Up gap**   | Next open is **above** the previous close |
| **Down gap** | Next open is **below** the previous close |

* A gap is **filled** when price returns to the pre-gap level.
* Gaps signal that something important changed in the **fundamentals** or **crowd psychology**.
* *Note:* the lecture's line on filled gaps is garbled. The usual rule is that an unfilled gap tends to be followed by price continuing in the gap's direction.

### The Four Gap Types

| Feature        | Common                       | Breakaway                                        | Runaway (Continuation / Measuring)  | Exhaustion                                       |
| -------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Location**   | Sideways or quiet market     | Breakout from a congestion area or pattern       | Middle of a trend                   | End of a move                                    |
| **Volume**     | Around average               | **Significantly high** (about 2x in the example) | **Very high** (2 to 3x average)     | **Low** before the gap, then **spikes** after it |
| **Filled?**    | Yes, quickly (within days)   | Rarely                                           | Usually **not**                     | Yes, **quickly**                                 |
| **Importance** | Low                          | **High**                                         | High                                | **High**                                         |
| **Meaning**    | Supply and demand in balance | Start of a **new trend**                         | Trend **continues**, gives a target | Trend **reversal**                               |

### Details by Type

#### Common Gap

* Forms in sideways ranges, for example inside a symmetrical triangle.
* Volume stays near normal and the gap closes within days.
* **Not important for trading.**

#### Breakaway Gap

* Price breaks out of a congestion area or chart pattern (rectangle, wedge and so on).
* The breakout point becomes **support** in an uptrend and **resistance** in a downtrend.
* **Action:** buy on an up gap, sell on a down gap.
* A breakaway gap combined with a chart-pattern breakout is a **higher-probability trade**.
* It is the **easiest to identify** and **most profitable** type to trade.

#### Runaway Gap

* Appears mid-trend during rapid, straight-line advances or declines.
* **Uptrend cause:** significant news that creates new buying interest.
* **Downtrend cause:** heavy liquidation of positions.
* **Measuring gap:** it sits roughly at the trend's midpoint, so the move's **target ≈ distance from trend start to gap, projected forward again**.

#### Exhaustion Gap

* Occurs at the **end** of a rapid move.

* **Uptrend:** a gap up is not followed by a new higher high.

* **Downtrend:** a gap down is not followed by a new lower low.

* **Volume is the key signal:**

  2. Volume is low while price keeps moving.
  4. Volume spikes once the gap opens, which confirms exhaustion.

* **Psychology:** bullish euphoria, then profit-taking, then demand dries up, then a sudden reversal on high volume.

* It is highly attractive and profitable to trade.

#### Island Reversal

* Forms when price **gaps back in the opposite direction** after an exhaustion gap, leaving an "island" of price action behind.
* It is a **strong confirmation** of the reversal, because the opposing forces are strong enough to gap against the prior trend.

## 6. Quick Trading Cheat Sheet

* Ignore **common** gaps.
* Trade **breakaway** gaps in the breakout direction.
* Use **runaway** gaps to hold the trend and project a target.
* Watch for **exhaustion** gaps plus a volume spike, and prepare for a reversal.
* Treat an **island reversal** as confirmation.
* **Always check volume** to identify the gap type.
